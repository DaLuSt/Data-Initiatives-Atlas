## Description

CEN is one of the three European Standardisation Organisations recognised by
Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 as providers of European standards, alongside
[[EU-CENELEC]] (electrotechnical) and [[EU-ETSI]] (ICT and
telecommunications). Confirmed by reading cencenelec.eu directly
(2026-08-28): CEN and CENELEC together are "two distinct private
international non-profit organizations" operating through **34 national
Member countries**, each represented by a national standardisation body,
with a wider network of "more than 200,000 technical experts from industry,
associations, public administrations, academia and societal organizations."
The Commission's own "Key players" page, also read directly, still states
"33 European countries" — the two primary sources disagree by one country,
most likely because the Commission page has not been updated since CEN's
membership last changed; both figures are recorded rather than silently
resolved.

Through Regulation 1025/2012 — the legal framework for the public-private
partnership between the ESOs and the EU/EFTA, confirmed by both cencenelec.eu
and the Commission page as the instrument under which the three
organisations "may receive a request to produce European harmonised
standards in support of EU legislation and policies" — CEN and its sibling
ESOs may be asked to produce harmonised European standards. Thirty percent of
European standards are stated (cencenelec.eu) to directly support EU
directives, giving presumption of conformity to the law.

## The NEN connection

[[NL-NEN]] is the Netherlands' national standardisation body, which makes it
one of the 33 CEN members by the composition rule above. The
`participates-in` relationship is recorded on `NL-NEN`, with evidence noting
that the membership is inferred from a sourced composition rule rather than
from a source naming NEN specifically — the same treatment applied to
[[NL-CBS]] in the ESS and [[NL-AP]] in the EDPB.

## Regulation 1025/2012 — now modelled

This section previously read *"Regulation 1025/2012 not modelled"*: the
regulation was described here but not created, because Batch 9 covered
organisations and standards and Batch 8's legislation sweep had not reached
it.

It is now [[EU-REG-1025-2012]], created on 2026-08-21 together with
[[EU-REG-223-2009]] — the other statutory base the Atlas had been describing
without modelling — so that the pair closes in one move. The `applies-to`
edges live on the regulation, which is the instrument doing the recognising.
