## Description

The treaty body of [[INTL-CONVENTION-108]]: the parties to the Convention,
sitting together.

## The Convention's answer to the EDPB

The Atlas holds [[EU-EDPB]], the board on which twelve national data
protection authorities sit and through which the GDPR's consistency
mechanism runs. T-PD is the older and wider counterpart — wider because
Convention 108's parties include eight states outside Europe entirely,
[[UY]], [[AR]], [[MX]], [[MA]], [[SN]], [[TN]], [[MU]] and [[CV]].

The two are **not** the same kind of body and no relationship between them
is asserted. The EDPB is an EU institution with binding dispute-resolution
powers over supervisory authorities; T-PD is a treaty committee of state
parties. What they share is the subject and, in the European cases, largely
the same governments.

## The body asking for ratifications nobody is sending

T-PD's most concrete current role in the Atlas is that it is the party
pressing for [[INTL-CONVENTION-108-PLUS]] to enter into force. It has
**strongly encouraged all state parties to sign and ratify as soon as
possible**, and the protocol still stands four ratifications short of the
38 it needs, nearly eight years after opening.

A treaty committee urging its own members to ratify its own protocol, for
eight years, is the shape of the problem — and it is visible in the Atlas
only because both the committee and the protocol are now entities.
