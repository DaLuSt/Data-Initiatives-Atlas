# Toetsingscommissie Inzet Bevoegdheden (TIB)

> **Sourcing caveat.** Compiled from search-engine results only; the cited
> pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. `verification:
> search-only`.

## Description

The TIB conducts **binding prior review** of whether the intended use of
certain special powers by [[NL-AIVD]] and [[NL-MIVD]] is lawful. The powers
in question are the intrusive ones the sources name explicitly: hacking
computers, intercepting telephone conversations, placing directional
microphones.

It is composed of **two judges or former judges and a technical expert** —
a composition that tells you what the review is for. It is a legality check
by people qualified to make one, not a policy or proportionality body.

## It reviews *after* the minister decides

This is the detail most easily got wrong. The TIB does not advise the
minister in advance of the decision; it reviews **after** the responsible
minister has already granted permission, and its decision is **binding**.
A minister's authorisation that the TIB finds unlawful does not take effect.

That makes the Dutch model unusually strong on paper compared with, for
instance, the French one: [[FR-CNCTR]] issues an *avis* to the Prime
Minister, who may proceed against it, with recourse to the Conseil d'État.

## The commission that delayed its own act

[[NL-WIV-2017]] was to enter into force on 1 January 2018. It entered into
force on **1 May 2018** instead, because it took longer than expected to
find candidates for the new TIB. The oversight body held up the statute
that created it — a fact worth recording because it is a rare case where the
Atlas can date an instrument's commencement to a staffing problem rather
than to a legislative one.

## Not modelled
