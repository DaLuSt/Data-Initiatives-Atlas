# Data Protection: SADC Model Law (2012)

> **Created 2026-10-23** (roadmap #552). The ITU's published text (about 137 000 characters, read in part: the foreword and the structure; Part III on the authority and Part XI on transborder flow). `start_date` is empty: only the month, November 2012, is given.

## What it says

Thirteen parts: definitions; scope; the Data Protection Authority; quality of data; general rules on processing; duties of the controller and processor; rights of the data subject; recourse to the judicial authority; sanctions; limitations; transborder flow; code of conduct; whistleblowing. Article 3: "An independent and administrative authority called the Protection Authority or Authority is established and will have oversight and control of this Model Law", composed of judges, a representative of the Prime Minister or Head of State, deputies and people appointed by human rights and ICT organisations. Articles 43 and 44: transfers to a Member State that transposed the law are allowed on a necessity test; to other recipients only if an adequate level of protection is ensured. The foreword places it in the HIPSSA project (December 2008 onwards), co-chaired by the African Union Commission and the ITU, with funding from the European Union's 9th European Development Fund.

## Not in the Atlas

Two companion HIPSSA model laws for SADC (on computer crime and cybercrime, and on electronic transactions and electronic commerce) exist per search results; their texts were not read. A MISA report says the data protection model law is being revised by SADC (secondary source, not recorded).
