# soft-law/

Entities of `type: soft-law` — non-binding instruments adopted by an
international or supranational body in a formal act: a recommendation,
resolution, declaration, compact or set of principles. They guide states or
institutions but create no legal obligation by themselves.

This is deliberately not `legislation/`. Nothing here is legislation, and the
`legislation` statistic counts only the legislation types.

What belongs here:

- A UNESCO Recommendation ([[UN-AI-ETHICS-RECOMMENDATION]]).
- A UN General Assembly resolution that sets out commitments rather than
  obligations ([[UN-2030-AGENDA]], [[UN-GDC]]).
- Principles adopted by a body and endorsed by a resolution ([[UN-FPOS]]).

What does not belong here:

- A binding instrument — a treaty or convention is an `agreement`.
- A national government's own plan or position — that is a `policy` or a
  `strategy` (for example `NL-PAS-TOE-OF-LEG-UIT`).
- A measurement or governance framework, which is a `framework`, even when
  a UN body supervises it ([[UN-SDG-INDICATORS]]).
- A technical specification, which is a `standard`.
