# Décret du 1er juin 2005 sur l'identification nationale (Haïti)

> **Created 2026-10-11** (roadmap #470, Haiti). The decree's full text was read directly on the ONI's own website; its title is not shown on that page (the heading is only "DÉCRET"), so this entity is named by its date and subject. The text read carries no date of publication in *Le Moniteur*.

## What it does

Issued by the provisional Executive at a time when, in its own words, "le pouvoir Législatif est, pour le moment, inopérant" and the Executive therefore legislates by decree. It introduces the **Carte d'Identification Nationale (CIN)** as the compulsory means of personal identification and the **Registre National d'Identification**.

- **Article 1:** identification of every natural person living on the national territory is compulsory; Haitians are identified by the national identification card, resident foreigners by their residence permit, foreign diplomats by a diplomatic card.
- **Article 2:** every Haitian receives, from birth and registration at the Office National d'Identification, a **national identification number** kept for life; it identifies sex, date and place of birth and the order of entry in the Registre National d'Identification.
- **Articles 3-4:** the card is issued from the age of eighteen, the first issue is free, it is renewed every ten years, and it carries the holder's data together with a digital photograph, scanned fingerprints and signature.
- **Articles 19-24:** the ONI is run by a board (Conseil d'Administration) with representatives of the justice, interior and finance ministries and others, and a Director General appointed by presidential order.

The preamble cites the decree of 21 September 1987 "créant l'Office National d'Identification des personnes physiques", so the Office predates this decree; this one sets its present structure and tasks.

## What was not found

No provision on data protection, privacy or confidentiality of the register appears in the text read. A separate data-protection measure is mentioned by secondary sources (an arrêté published in *Le Moniteur* on 15 May 2018, a 2017 bill) but neither was read, and the Atlas records no Haitian data-protection act.
