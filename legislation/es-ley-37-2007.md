# Ley 37/2007 — re-use of public sector information

> **Verified 2026-08-26.** Three of four cited pages were read directly:
> BOE's own texts of both the 2007 act and the 2021 decree-law, and
> ECIJA's law-firm analysis. The datos.gob.es validation post was not
> re-fetched.

## Description

Ley 37/2007 governs the re-use of public sector information in Spain. It
predates [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]] by twelve years and became its
transposition by being **amended in place** in 2021.

Confirmed by reading boe.es's own 2007 text directly: the original act's
stated aim was to "harmonise the exploitation of information in the public
sector" and "facilitate the creation of products and services based on
public sector documents," covering state, regional and local
administration documents while excluding material subject to third-party
intellectual property, national defence, and documents requiring a
legitimate legal interest to access.

**Real Decreto-ley 24/2021 of 2 November** modified ten articles, one
additional provision, the sole transitory provision and the annex, and added
three new articles. The substantive changes:

- **scope extended** to certain public undertakings (`sociedades
  mercantiles públicas`) — public air transport and energy companies among
  them — and to **research data**;
- documents to be supplied **electronically in open, accessible, findable
  and reusable formats**, with metadata, under an **"open by design and by
  default"** principle confirmed by reading ecija.com directly;
- **dynamic data** available immediately after collection via APIs and,
  where appropriate, bulk download;
- **gratuity for reuse**, with limited exceptions — also confirmed by
  reading ecija.com directly, and not previously recorded here;
- the concept of **high-value datasets** introduced.

## This closes a gap that had spread across two countries

`progress/backlog.md` has carried this item since the Belgium batch:

> *The Open Data Directive transpositions for Belgium and France. Neither
> identified. Both countries have a well-known **earlier** open data act
> (2016 in both cases) that looks like the answer and chronologically cannot
> be it.*

Spain has exactly the same trap and it is the same shape — a well-known
earlier act, here from **2007**, even further from 2019 than the Belgian and
French 2016 acts. The difference is that for Spain the sources say plainly
what happened to it.

| Germany | [[DE-DNG]] |

Three of five are now closed. The Belgian and French gaps stay open and stay
labelled as gaps, because finding Spain's answer says nothing about theirs.

**The 2016-act trap is now confirmed as a research hazard rather than a
coincidence**, which is worth more than the single closed link: in three
countries out of five, the obvious candidate is a chronologically impossible
one, and in the third the real answer was an amendment to something older
still.

## An omnibus transposition, of which the Atlas sees one eighth

RDL 24/2021 is not an open data instrument. It transposes **eight EU
directives at once** — covered bonds, cross-border distribution of
collective investment undertakings, open data and PSI re-use, certain online
transmissions of copyright works, temporary import exemptions, and others.

Spain reached for a decree-law because the **transposition deadline of
17 July had already passed** and a **letter of formal notice arrived on
29 September** — the first stage of infringement proceedings.

Two consequences for the Atlas, both recorded rather than resolved:

1. **The amending instrument is not modelled as an entity.** RDL 24/2021 is
   cited as a source and named in the evidence string, but creating it would
   mean creating an entity seven-eighths of whose content is outside the
   Atlas's scope. The `implements-requirement-from` edge therefore runs from
   the amended act, which is the instrument that actually carries the
   obligations today.
2. **The infringement pressure is not modelled.** That a transposition was
   late, and produced under threat of proceedings, is a fact about how EU
   law lands in a member state which the Atlas has no vocabulary for. It
   sits in this prose only. [[ES-LCGC]] shows the same pressure at an
   earlier stage.

Germany's [[DE-NIS2UMSUCG]] → [[DE-BSIG]] raised the amendment question and
France's [[FR-LIL]] raised it again. Spain adds the third variant: an
**omnibus** amending instrument. The Atlas's answer in all three cases has
been to keep the amended act as the entity and put the amending act in the
evidence. That is now a consistent, three-country practice rather than an
improvisation, and it is worth writing into `metadata/relationship-types.md`
when the amendment relationship type is finally decided.
