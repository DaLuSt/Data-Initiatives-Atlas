# Law of Electronic Signatures and Transactions No. 78 of 2012 (Iraq)

> **Created 2026-10-11** (roadmap #471, Iraq). The full English translation published by the Ministry of Justice's Gazette Office (2019) was read; the translation is not the authentic Arabic text.

## What it does

Issued by the President on 18 October 2012 and published in Official Gazette issue 4256 of **5 November 2012**. Its aims (Article 2) are to provide the legal frame for electronic means in transactions, to give legal conclusiveness to the electronic signature and electronic transactions, and to enhance confidence in them. It applies to electronic transactions by natural or legal persons and to electronic financial and commercial securities (Article 3), and excludes personal status, wills and endowments, transactions on immovable property (other than rental contracts), court procedures and documents that must be authenticated by a notary.

An electronic signature has the same conclusiveness as a written one in civil, commercial and administrative transactions if it meets the conditions of Article 5. The Minister of Communication and "the Company" — defined in Article 1 as the general company of international information network services in the Ministry of Communication — have the roles: under Article 6 the Company grants authentication certificates after the Minister's approval, defines technical standards for e-signature systems and supervises the certification authorities.

## What is not here

This is not a data-protection law, and the Atlas records no Iraqi data-protection act: none was found in a primary source. Secondary sources report that the Ministry never issued the instructions Article 5 anticipates and that an official e-signature system was announced in September 2025; neither was read.
