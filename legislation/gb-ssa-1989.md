# Security Service Act 1989

> **Sourcing caveat.** Compiled from search-engine results only; the cited
> pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. `verification:
> search-only`.

## Description

The 1989 act **placed** [[GB-MI5]] on a statutory basis and enabled certain
actions to be taken on the authority of **warrants issued by the Secretary
of State**.

## "Placed on a statutory basis", not "established"

The phrasing in the sources is exact and worth preserving. MI5 existed long
before 1989; the act did not create it. It **avowed** it — acknowledged the
service in statute, defined it, and gave its intrusive actions a legal
footing.

That is a different legislative act from what happened in the Netherlands,
Belgium, Spain and Poland, where the statute constitutes the service. It is
closest to Germany's position, where the services predate their current acts
too, but the German acts are conventional constituting statutes.

The UK avowed its services **one at a time**: MI5 in 1989, then [[GB-SIS]]
and [[GB-GCHQ]] together in [[GB-ISA-1994]].

## Ministerial warrants, with the independent check arriving 27 years later

Under this act the authorising authority is the **Secretary of State**. No
judge, no commission, no prior independent review.

[[GB-IPCO]] — the independent check — arrives only with [[GB-IPA-2016]].
Between 1989 and 2016 the UK's arrangement rested on ministerial warrants
plus, from 1994, a parliamentary committee.

Contrast [[NL-WIV-2017]], which put the services, the binding prior reviewer
and the retrospective reviewer in one instrument, and Belgium, which
legislated oversight **before** the services ([[BE-TOEZICHTSWET-1991]],
seven years before [[BE-WIV-1998]]).
