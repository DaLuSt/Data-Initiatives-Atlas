# Organisatiewet Kadaster

> **Created 2026-09-06**, closing a gap [[NL-KADASTER]] left implicit:
> the act constituting it as a body — as distinct from
> [[NL-KADASTERWET]], which governs the registers it maintains — was
> not itself an Atlas entity. wetten.overheid.nl's own text was read
> directly.

## Description

Confirmed by reading wetten.overheid.nl's own text directly: "Wet van 14
februari 1994, houdende verzelfstandiging van de Rijksdienst van het
Kadaster en de Openbare Registers" (Act of 14 February 1994 on the
autonomisation of the State Cadastral Service and Public Registers) —
the full title matching the Organisatiewet Kadaster's colloquial name.

**Article 2(1)**, quoted verbatim: "Er is een Dienst voor het kadaster en
de openbare registers. De Dienst is werkzaam in het Europese deel van
Nederland en in de openbare lichamen Bonaire, Sint Eustatius en Saba. Hij
bezit rechtspersoonlijkheid en is gevestigd te Apeldoorn" (There is a
Cadastre and Public Registers Service. The Service operates in the
European part of the Netherlands and in the public bodies Bonaire, Sint
Eustatius and Saba. It possesses legal personality and is based in
Apeldoorn). **Article 2(2)** assigns the Service the tasks given to it
under [[NL-KADASTERWET]] and the Kadasterwet BES. **Article 3** gives the
Service both a management board (bestuur) and a supervisory board (raad
van toezicht) — the zelfstandig-bestuursorgaan structure Wikipedia had
already dated to 1994 on [[NL-KADASTER]], now with a primary statutory
citation.

## The pair, now both modelled

[[NL-KADASTERWET]] (1989) governs the **registers** — the cadastral and
topographic base registrations maintained as authentic data. This entity
(1994) constitutes the **body** that maintains them. The five-year gap
between the two dates is itself informative: Kadaster operated under its
registers act for five years before being given its own legal
personality.

## Not modelled

- The **Kadasterwet BES**, the Caribbean-Netherlands equivalent named in
  Article 2(2).
- Articles beyond 2 and 3 — the Act's governance detail (board
  appointment, supervisory powers) is not researched here.
