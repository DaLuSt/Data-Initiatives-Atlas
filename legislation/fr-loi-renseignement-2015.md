# Loi du 24 juillet 2015 relative au renseignement

> **Sourcing caveat.** Compiled from search-engine results only; the cited
> pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. `verification:
> search-only`.

## Description

The 2015 intelligence law, codified as **Book VIII of the Code de la
sécurité intérieure**, is the single instrument governing all four French
services in the Atlas. It legislates three things:

1. **Which techniques** may be used — metadata access, categories of
   interception, and more intrusive devices capturing words and images in a
   private place or collecting computer data.
2. **Which purposes** may legally justify their use.
3. **Who authorises them** — the Prime Minister, after an opinion from
   [[FR-CNCTR]].

## A law about techniques, not about services

This is the structural fact that shapes the whole French cluster. The act
does not constitute [[FR-DGSE]], [[FR-DGSI]], [[FR-DRM]] or [[FR-DRSD]];
it defines a catalogue of powers and designates who may reach into it.

The consequence for the graph is that all four services point at this one
entity. Germany's cluster is three parallel pairs; France's is a star.

[[GB-IPA-2016]] makes the same choice in the UK, and [[DE-G10]] does it for
one specific right in Germany.

## Codification: this entity is both the act and Book VIII

The Atlas holds **one** entity for the *loi* and its codified form. French
law routinely dissolves an act into a code, and modelling the two separately
would create a duplicate with no way to keep them consistent.

The frontmatter records "Code de la sécurité intérieure, Livre VIII" as an
alternative name so that a search for either resolves here. This follows the
treatment already given to [[FR-LIL]], which the Atlas holds as one entity
across its 1978 original and its 2018 GDPR-conformity rewrite.

## Appeal to the Conseil d'État

The act opens a right of appeal to the **Conseil d'État**, available to any
citizen with an interest in acting. [[FR-CNCTR]] may also refer a matter
there when it believes an authorisation was granted irregularly. The court
may annul the decision, compensate the applicant, **order the destruction of
collected data**, and refer the matter to the public prosecutor.

## Not modelled

- The **law of 30 July 2021** on the prevention of terrorist acts and on
  intelligence, which amended this regime.
- The **decree of 12 May 2014, revised 14 June 2017**, defining the
  intelligence community, and the **Code de la défense** provisions the
  sources say now inscribe it.
- The **second circle** of services, which the sources say may also access
  intelligence techniques.
- The **Conseil d'État** and the **délégation parlementaire au renseignement**.
