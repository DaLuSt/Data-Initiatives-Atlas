# Justice and Security Act 2013

> **Verified 2026-08-22.** The statute text at legislation.gov.uk was read
> directly and confirmed § 1, § 2 and the exact enactment date below.

## Description

Confirmed directly on legislation.gov.uk (2026-08-22), § 1(1)-(2): "There
is to be a body known as the Intelligence and Security Committee of
Parliament... The ISC is to consist of nine members who are to be drawn
both from the members of the House of Commons and from the members of the
House of Lords." Enacted **25 April 2013**. Part 1 of the 2013 act re-founded [[GB-ISC]] as a statutory committee and
provides for oversight of [[GB-MI5]], [[GB-SIS]], [[GB-GCHQ]] and other
government activities relating to intelligence or security matters.

## One word wider

[[GB-ISA-1994]] gave the ISC the function of examining the agencies'
**expenditure, administration and policy**. This act gives it expenditure,
administration, policy **and operations**.

A committee limited to the first three scrutinises how an agency is *run*.
Adding *operations* lets it scrutinise what the agency *does*. It is the
single most consequential difference between the 1994 and 2013 arrangements,
and it is one word.

## `references`, not `supersedes`

The relationship to [[GB-ISA-1994]] is `references`. The 1994 act is still
in force and still constitutes [[GB-SIS]] and [[GB-GCHQ]]; only its
oversight provision was displaced.

`supersedes` in this repository asserts that one instrument has replaced
another, and carries the expectation that the target takes
`status: superseded`. That would be wrong here — and the same reasoning is
recorded on [[GB-ISA-1994]] and on [[GB-IPA-2016]], which amends the 1994
act through Schedule 10.

Three UK acts touching one another without any of them retiring another is
unusual enough in the Atlas to be worth stating three times.

## Not modelled

- The act's **Part 2**, on closed material procedures in civil proceedings —
  the more litigated half of the statute, and unrelated to the Atlas's
  subject matter.
- The ISC's **memorandum of understanding** with the Prime Minister, which
  in practice defines the operational remit.
