# Intelligence Services Act 1994

## Description

The 1994 act established and regulated [[GB-SIS]] and [[GB-GCHQ]],
providing a statutory basis for their activities and, in the sources' words,
ensuring accountability and legal oversight. It sets out GCHQ's function as
a **foreign-focused signals intelligence agency**.

## Two services in one act, because they share a minister

The pairing follows the ministerial line, not the discipline. SIS and GCHQ
both answer to the Foreign Secretary; [[GB-MI5]] answers to the Home
Secretary and got its own act, [[GB-SSA-1989]], five years earlier.

Human intelligence and signals intelligence therefore sit in the same
statute — a grouping no other country in this batch makes.

## The oversight body it created has since been re-founded

The 1994 act established the **Intelligence and Security Committee**, a body
of parliamentarians examining the agencies' *expenditure, administration and
policy*.

[[GB-JSA-2013]] Part 1 then re-founded the ISC on a new statutory basis with
a wider remit that includes **operations**.

The Atlas points [[GB-ISC]]'s `governed-by` edge at the 2013 act and records
the 1994 origin here in prose. **No `supersedes` relationship is asserted**
between the two acts: the 1994 act remains in force and continues to
constitute [[GB-SIS]] and [[GB-GCHQ]]. Only its oversight provision was
displaced, and the Atlas has no relationship type for "partially superseded"
— asserting `supersedes` would falsely retire a live statute.
