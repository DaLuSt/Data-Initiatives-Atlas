## Description

The **only legally binding international treaty on the protection of
personal data**, and the instrument European data protection actually starts
from.

## What this entity corrects

Before it existed, the Atlas presented [[EU-GDPR]] as the origin of European
data protection. It is not. Convention 108 was opened for signature on **28
January 1981** and entered into force on **1 October 1985** — thirty-five
years before the GDPR became applicable, and thirteen years before the Data
Protection Directive it replaced.

The principles the GDPR is built on — fair and lawful processing, purpose
limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security,
special protection for sensitive data, rights of access and rectification —
are in the 1981 text. The GDPR elaborated and enforced them. It did not
invent them.

**28 January is Data Protection Day** across Europe for exactly this reason:
it is the anniversary of this treaty's opening for signature.

## The one instrument in the Atlas that is not regional

Every other binding instrument the Atlas holds is either an EU act, binding
27 states, or a national act binding one. Convention 108 is **open to
accession by any state**, and eight non-European states have acceded:

## ⚠ The European parties are deliberately not enumerated

Sources state that **all Council of Europe member states are parties** to
Convention 108. If that rule holds, roughly 46 more `applies-in` edges are
owed.

They are **not asserted**, for two reasons. The one source found for the
rule gives a stale member count — it says 47, which has been wrong since
[[RU]]'s expulsion in 2022 — and [[RU]]'s own position as a party after
expulsion from the organisation is not something any source found addresses.
Second, generating 46 edges from a rule whose supporting source is visibly
out of date would be mass rather than knowledge.

The Council of Europe's chart of signatures and ratifications is the
document that settles it, and the egress proxy still blocks `coe.int` as of
this pass (2026-08-28) — every `coe.int` and `rm.coe.int` path tried
returned HTTP 403. This is recorded in `discovery/research-queue.md` as a
re-verification-blocked item rather than guessed at.

## The GDPR points back at it

[[EU-GDPR]] **Recital 105** provides that the Commission shall take account
of a third country's accession to Convention 108 when assessing the adequacy
of its protection.

That is a live legal channel, not a historical footnote: accession to this
treaty is a factor in EU adequacy decisions, which is why states with no
prospect of EU membership accede to it. [[EU-UK-ADEQUACY]] is the adequacy
instrument the Atlas already holds.

## The two protocols

- [[INTL-CONVENTION-108-PROTOCOL]] (ETS 181, 2001) added independent
  supervisory authorities and adequacy-based transfer rules.
- [[INTL-CONVENTION-108-PLUS]] (CETS 223, 2018) modernises the Convention
  and is **not yet in force**.
