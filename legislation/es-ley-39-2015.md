# Ley 39/2015 — Procedimiento Administrativo Común

> **Added 2026-09-04, `verification: primary-source` from creation.**
> `discovery/research-queue.md` had queued this law, alongside
> [[ES-LEY-40-2015]], as Spain's current legal base for much of what
> [[ES-AEAD]] does and as the successor to the 2007 law that first gave
> Spain an electronic-administration regime. `boe.es`'s own consolidated
> text was read directly this pass.

## Description

Ley 39/2015 governs the common administrative procedure that every
Spanish public administration must follow in its dealings with
citizens — published in the BOE on **2 October 2015**, in force from
**2 October 2016**. It was enacted the same day as its organisational
counterpart, [[ES-LEY-40-2015]]: together the pair replaced the general
framework the 1992 administrative-procedure law had provided.

## What it replaced

Ley 39/2015's own sole repealing provision revokes **Ley 11/2007, de
acceso electrónico de los ciudadanos a los Servicios Públicos**
(LAECSP) — Spain's original e-government law, and the statute
[[ES-ENI]]'s own body once named as the ENI's now-repealed origin. Ley
11/2007 is not itself an Atlas entity: it is fully superseded and its
provisions live on, absorbed and expanded, in Ley 39/2015 and
[[ES-LEY-40-2015]] — the same treatment [[DE-BSI]]'s file gives its own
1991 predecessor act.

## Interoperability, without naming the ENI

Reading the consolidated BOE text directly finds the law requires that
**"los registros electrónicos de todas y cada una de las
Administraciones, deberán ser plenamente interoperables"** (the
electronic registries of each and every Administration must be fully
interoperable) — but the text does not name the Esquema Nacional de
Interoperabilidad itself. [[ES-ENI]]'s own entity separately establishes,
via a different source (anabad.org), that the ENI is contemplated in
Article 156 of [[ES-LEY-40-2015]], not this law. No relationship to
[[ES-ENI]] is asserted from this entity for the same reason [[ES-ENI]]'s
own file gives: the sources describe a general procedural duty and a
statutory contemplation, not a typed dependency the Atlas's vocabulary
distinguishes cleanly.
