# Loi n° 2007-1443 du 9 octobre 2007 portant création d'une délégation parlementaire au renseignement

> **Created 2026-09-06**, closing a gap [[FR-CNCTR]] flagged explicitly in
> its own "Not modelled" section: France's parliamentary oversight body,
> the counterpart to [[DE-PKGR]], [[GB-ISC]] and [[PL-KSS]], was absent —
> making France look like it has judicial-style control and no
> parliamentary control, "not an accurate picture of French oversight."
> `legifrance.gouv.fr` was read directly and agreed, verbatim on the key
> points, with the Assemblée nationale's own page.

## Description

Establishes the **délégation parlementaire au renseignement (DPR)**, a
joint body shared between the National Assembly and the Senate. Confirmed
by reading legifrance.gouv.fr's own text of the law directly: the DPR
comprises **eight members** — four deputies and four senators — plus the
presidents of each chamber's permanent committee for internal security and
defence, who sit ex officio. Its mandate is to monitor "the general
activity and means of specialised services" placed under the ministers
responsible for internal security, defence, the economy and the budget —
a broader ministerial span than [[FR-CNCTR]]'s six named services, though
the Atlas models the same four services under both bodies (see
[[FR-DPR]]).

**What it may not see**: operational activities, government instructions
to the services, and exchanges with foreign intelligence services are all
explicitly excluded from the information ministers must provide. Members
and the delegation's own staff hold security clearances under Penal Code
Article 413-9, are bound by national-defence secrecy, and the delegation
publishes annual reports while protecting classified material.

## Not modelled

- The **précise 2007 legislative history and later amendments** — the
  DPR's remit and composition have been adjusted since 2007 (e.g. Assemblée
  nationale pages for later legislatures show renewed compositions); only
  the 2007 founding act's own text is cited here.
