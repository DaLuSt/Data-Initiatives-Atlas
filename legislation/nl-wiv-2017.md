## Description

The Wiv 2017 is the legal framework for [[NL-AIVD]] and [[NL-MIVD]]. Its
own official citation page, read directly on `wetten.overheid.nl`, gives the
statute's identifier as **BWBR0039896**, signed 26 July 2017 (Stb. 2017,
317). The sources describe it as establishing **precisely** the tasks of
the services and the exercise of their powers. It replaced the Wiv 2002.

Two things it did:

- **Extended interception to cable.** The services gained
  onderzoeksopdrachtgerichte interceptie (OOG) authority to examine *wired*
  telecommunications, not only wireless — the change that gave the act its
  popular name, the **sleepwet** ("dragnet act").
- **Built its own oversight.** It regulates prior assessment by [[NL-TIB]]
  and subsequent (now also, under [[NL-TWCO]], partly real-time) oversight
  by [[NL-CTIVD]] in the same instrument that grants the powers.

## Entry into force, precisely

`wetten.overheid.nl`'s own citation page, read directly, gives a more exact
sequence than "delayed to 1 May 2018":

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 26 July 2017 | Act signed (Stb. 2017, 317) |
| 1 September 2017 | Provisions needed for recruitment and security clearances take effect (Stb. 2017, 318) |
| **1 May 2018** | Remainder of the act enters into force (Stb. 2018, 119) |

Entry into force had originally been set for 1 January 2018. It slipped
because — per the Dutch Wikipedia article and the dedicated article on
[[NL-TIB]], both read directly this pass — the new [[NL-TIB]] itself did
not become operational until 1 April 2018, one month before the wider act
took effect, in part to align its working method with European case-law
requirements on prior authorisation.

## The 2018 referendum

The Wikipedia article, read directly, confirms a **consultative referendum
on 21 March 2018**: turnout 51.54% (above the 30% validity threshold), with
49.44% against and 46.53% in favour. The government's response included
stricter data-sharing protocols with foreign services and annual ministerial
review of retained intercepted data. This detail was previously recorded
without confirmation; it is now sourced directly.

## Currently modified by a temporary act

[[NL-TWCO]] deviates from this act's regime for investigations into
countries with an offensive cyber programme, in force since 1 July 2024 and
expiring four years later. Any statement about what the Dutch services may
currently do has to read the two together.

## No `applies-in` [[NL]], and why

Some national instruments in the Atlas carry `applies-in` to their own
country. This one deliberately does not — an edge asserting only that a
Dutch act applies in the Netherlands would give `applies-in` a second
meaning alongside its real one, the EU-instrument-reaches-member-state case
that makes the Atlas country-neutral. The entity reaches [[NL]] anyway,
through [[NL-AIVD]] and [[NL-BZK]].

What is worth saying is the thing the missing edge might otherwise be
mistaken for: **no EU instrument carries `applies-in` to the Dutch
intelligence services**, and none should — see
[[DOMAIN-NATIONAL-SECURITY]].

## Not modelled
