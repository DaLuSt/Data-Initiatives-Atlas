# Wet op de inlichtingen- en veiligheidsdiensten 2017 (Wiv 2017)

> **Sourcing caveat.** Compiled from search-engine results only; the cited
> pages were confirmed to exist but were not read — `wetten.overheid.nl` is
> blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so **no consolidated statute
> text is cited**. `verification: search-only`.

## Description

The Wiv 2017 is the legal framework for [[NL-AIVD]] and [[NL-MIVD]]. The
sources describe it as establishing **precisely** the tasks of the services
and the exercise of their powers. It replaced the Wiv 2002.

Two things it did:

- **Extended interception to cable.** The services gained authority to
  examine *wired* telecommunications, not only wireless — the change that
  gave the act its popular name, the **sleepwet** ("dragnet act").
- **Built its own oversight.** It regulates prior assessment by [[NL-TIB]]
  and subsequent oversight by [[NL-CTIVD]] in the same instrument that
  grants the powers.

## The act that its own oversight body delayed

Entry into force was set for 1 January 2018 and happened on **1 May 2018**,
because finding candidates for the new [[NL-TIB]] took longer than expected.

## Currently modified by a temporary act

[[NL-TWCO]] deviates from this act's regime for investigations into
countries with an offensive cyber programme, in force since 1 July 2024 and
expiring four years later. Any statement about what the Dutch services may
currently do has to read the two together.

## No `applies-in` [[NL]], and why

Some national instruments in the Atlas carry `applies-in` to their own
country. This one deliberately does not.

That pattern was reviewed in the preceding batch and recorded in
`progress/backlog.md` as something to **reconsider rather than extend**: an
edge asserting only that a Dutch act applies in the Netherlands gives
`applies-in` a second meaning alongside its real one, which is the
EU-instrument-reaches-member-state case that makes the Atlas
country-neutral.

The entity reaches [[NL]] anyway, through [[NL-AIVD]] and [[NL-BZK]].

What is worth saying is the thing the missing edge might otherwise be
mistaken for: **no EU instrument carries `applies-in` to the Dutch
intelligence services**, and none should — see
[[DOMAIN-NATIONAL-SECURITY]].
