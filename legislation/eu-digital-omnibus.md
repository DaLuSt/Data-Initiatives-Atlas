# Digital Omnibus (proposal)

## ⚠ This is a proposal, not law

`status: proposed`. The European Commission introduced the Digital Omnibus
Regulation proposal on **19 November 2025**. It has been sent to the
European Parliament and the Council for review, amendment and adoption.
Adoption is reported as expected around the end of 2026, with resulting
reforms taking effect around mid-2027 to 2028.

**Nothing in the Atlas is marked superseded because of this proposal.**
[[EU-DGA]] and [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]] both remain `status: active`.

## Description

The proposal aims to simplify the EU digital legal framework. Its
most consequential elements for the Atlas:

- **Repeal and consolidation.** It would completely repeal [[EU-DGA]], the
  [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]] and the Free Flow of Non-Personal Data
  Regulation, transferring those instruments into [[EU-DATA-ACT]].
- **GDPR amendment.** It would amend [[EU-GDPR]], the ePrivacy Directive and
  the Data Act. A widely-reported element is a new lawful basis of
  legitimate interest for processing personal data when developing or
  operating AI systems, subject to safeguards.
- **AI Act timing.** Some [[EU-AI-ACT]] obligations are reported to have
  been postponed following an AI-related omnibus amendment.

Reception has been contested: the proposal is reported to face strong
opposition from data protection authorities, NGOs and academics,
particularly over the GDPR changes.

## Why this entity exists

A knowledge graph of data legislation that shows only enacted law gives a
false picture of stability. Three of the EU entities in this Atlas are
currently proposed for repeal, and a reader looking at [[EU-DGA]] in
isolation would have no way to know that. Recording the proposal — clearly
marked as such, with `confidence: low` and a purpose-built relationship type
— makes the pending change visible without asserting it has happened.

## Sourcing weakness

The only official citation is the CELEX reference for the proposal document.
Every substantive detail comes from law-firm and academic commentary, which
sits low in the README's source preference order. The specific scope of
amendments should be read from the proposal text itself before being relied
upon, and the proposal's *current legislative status* — which may have moved
since November 2025 — is unverified.
