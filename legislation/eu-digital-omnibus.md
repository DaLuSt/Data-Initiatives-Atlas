introduced the Digital Omnibus proposal on **19 November 2025**. Confirmed

## Description

The proposal aims to simplify the EU digital legal framework. Confirmed by
reading whitecase.com and medialaws.eu directly, its most consequential
elements for the Atlas:

- **Repeal and consolidation.** It would completely repeal [[EU-DGA]], the
  [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]] and the Free Flow of Non-Personal Data
  Regulation, transferring those instruments into [[EU-DATA-ACT]] via new
  chapters — including a shift in the public-sector access-regime
  criterion from "exceptional need" to "public emergencies."
- **GDPR amendment.** It would amend [[EU-GDPR]] and the ePrivacy
  Directive. Confirmed elements include redefining "personal data" around
  reasonably-likely identifiability, a new lawful basis permitting
  residual processing of sensitive data categories during AI development
  under safeguards, a raised breach-notification threshold (high-risk
  only, 96-hour deadline via a single-entry point), and streamlined
  information duties.
- **Data Act amendment.** Strengthened trade-secret protections, adapted
  cloud-switching rules for SMEs, and a new chapter prohibiting unjustified
  data localisation.

Reception has been contested: the proposal is reported to face strong
opposition from data protection authorities, NGOs and academics,
particularly over the GDPR changes.

## Why this entity exists

A knowledge graph of data legislation that shows only enacted law gives a
false picture of stability. Two of the EU entities in this Atlas
([[EU-DGA]] and [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]]) are currently proposed for
repeal by this instrument, and a reader looking at either in isolation
would have no way to know that. Recording the proposal — clearly marked as
such, with a purpose-built relationship type — makes the pending change
visible without asserting it has happened.
