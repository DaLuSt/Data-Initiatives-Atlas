# Informācijas atklātības likums (Freedom of Information Law)

> **Created 2026-09-25**, closing the Latvian half of the scoping gap
> `discovery/unresolved.md` item #44 flagged: Latvia (and Bulgaria, now
> [[BG-ZDOI]]) lacked a modelled transposing instrument for the
> [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]], even though both were named among the four
> member states referred to the CJEU over it in February 2023. Sourced
> from likumi.lv (Latvia's own official legal-acts portal), a European
> Commission subdomain's own news page, and an independent Latvian news
> account, all read directly.

## Description

Latvia's Informācijas atklātības likums (Freedom of Information Law)
was **adopted 29 October 1998** and entered into force **20 November
1998**, governing access to and re-use of information held by public
bodies. Confirmed directly on likumi.lv's own text: the act's own
"Informatīva atsauce uz Eiropas Savienības direktīvām" section names
**Directive (EU) 2019/1024** (the Open Data Directive) among the EU
instruments it incorporates — Latvia's transposing instrument, unlike
Bulgaria's, is a long-standing general information-access law amended
piecemeal, not a single named transposition act.

**Amendments in force from 13 June 2022** (dated 19 May 2022) added
provisions on high-value datasets and dynamic data — the specific
categories the Open Data Directive introduces beyond the earlier PSI
Directive.

## Referred to the CJEU regardless

Confirmed directly on a European Commission subdomain's own news page,
independently corroborated by a Latvian Public Broadcasting (LSM) news
account: the Commission referred **Belgium, Bulgaria, Latvia and the
Netherlands** to the Court of Justice of the European Union on
**15 February 2023** for failing to transpose the Open Data Directive,
following the 17 July 2021 deadline, a September 2021 letter of formal
notice, and an April–June 2022 reasoned opinion. Unlike Bulgaria's later
fix, Latvia's own June 2022 amendment **predates** the referral —
raising the question of why the Commission still judged transposition
incomplete at the time of referral, which no source read this pass
answers.

## Not modelled

- Why the June 2022 amendment, which addresses the directive's own
  high-value-dataset and dynamic-data requirements, did not avert the
  February 2023 CJEU referral — no source read explains the gap.
- Whether the resulting CJEU case against Latvia was subsequently closed
  or withdrawn.
