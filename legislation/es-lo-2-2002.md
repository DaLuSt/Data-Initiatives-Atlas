# Ley Orgánica 2/2002, reguladora del control judicial previo del CNI

## Description

An organic act doing one job: amending the *Ley Orgánica del Poder Judicial*
so that [[ES-CNI]] activities affecting **Articles 18.2 and 18.3** of the
Spanish Constitution — inviolability of the home, and secrecy of
communications — require **prior judicial authorisation**. Confirmed by
reading the act's own text directly: it names a specific Supreme Court
magistrate (Sala Segunda de lo Penal or Tercera de lo Contencioso-
Administrativo), serving a **five-year term**, proposed by the **Consejo
General del Poder Judicial**, and requiring **three years' prior service**
at the Supreme Court.

## The shortest-scoped entity in the Atlas

Most law entities here do many things. This one exists to make a single
amendment possible, and its title says so: *reguladora del control judicial
previo del Centro Nacional de Inteligencia*.

It is `references` [[ES-LEY-11-2002]] rather than `implements` or
`implements-requirement-from`. The two are **complementary acts passed on
the same day**, not a higher instrument and its implementation:
[[ES-LEY-11-2002]] Article 12 points forward to this act, and this act
points back. `implements-requirement-from` is reserved in this repository
for the EU→national chain, and using it between two same-day domestic acts
would blur that.

## A judge, in advance

Spain's is the only regime in this batch where a **judge** authorises in
advance. The Netherlands has binding review by a commission of judges
([[NL-TIB]]) *after* the minister decides; France has an opinion to the
Prime Minister ([[FR-CNCTR]]); the UK, Germany, Belgium and Poland put the
initial decision elsewhere again.

Spain reached that position because its constitution forced the question:
touching Articles 18.2 and 18.3 required amending the judiciary's own
organic law, and once that was open the natural answer was a judge.
