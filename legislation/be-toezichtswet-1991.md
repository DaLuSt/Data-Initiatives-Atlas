## Description

The 1991 act regulates oversight of the Belgian police and intelligence
services and created **two standing committees at once**: Comité P for the
police and [[BE-COMITE-I]] for the intelligence and security services.
Confirmed by reading an unofficial German-language coordinated text of the
act directly (2026-08-26): Article 1 gives the oversight's object as the
protection of the constitutional and legal rights of persons, and the
coordination and effectiveness of the police and intelligence/security
services respectively, plus ensuring proper functioning of the
Coordination Unit for Threat Analysis (OCAD). Each committee has its own
attached investigation service.

## Belgium regulated the watchers before it regulated the watched

This act is **seven years older** than [[BE-WIV-1998]], the organic act
constituting [[BE-VSSE]] and [[BE-ADIV]].

That ordering is unique in this batch. Everywhere else the oversight
instrument is the later one, sometimes by decades:

| Country | Service act | Oversight act | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Belgium** | 1998 | **1991** | **−7 years** |
| Netherlands | [[NL-WIV-2017]] | same act | 0 |
| Germany | [[DE-BNDG]] etc. | [[DE-PKGRG]] | later |
| United Kingdom | [[GB-SSA-1989]] / [[GB-ISA-1994]] | [[GB-JSA-2013]], [[GB-IPA-2016]] | +19 to +27 years |

## Amended in 2006 for OCAD

The act was amended on **10 July 2006** so that OCAD — the Coordination Unit
for Threat Analysis, established that year — falls under the **joint**
supervision of Comité P and Comité I. OCAD is not an Atlas entity, so the
arrangement is recorded and not modelled.

## Not modelled

- **Comité P**, the police oversight committee this act also created.
- The act's own **amendment history** beyond 2006.
- The **role assigned to Comité I under [[BE-GDPR-WET]]** for data
  protection verification at the services, which comes from the 2018 act
  rather than from this one — see [[BE-VSSE]].
