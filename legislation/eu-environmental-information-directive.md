# Directive 2003/4/EC — public access to environmental information

> **Sourcing caveat.** This entity was compiled from search-engine results
> only; the cited pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. See
> `discovery/unresolved.md` and `progress/current-batch.md`.

## Description

Directive 2003/4/EC of **28 January 2003** guarantees the right of access to
environmental information held by or for public authorities, and requires
that such information be progressively made available and disseminated to
the public. Public authorities must supply the environmental information
they hold **to any natural or legal person on request**.

It **repealed Council Directive 90/313/EEC** of 7 June 1990 on freedom of
access to information on the environment, and its transposition deadline was
**14 February 2005**.

## The first EU instrument in the Atlas that implements a UN one

Every `implements-requirement-from` relationship in the Atlas has, until
now, run **from a national act to an EU instrument** — that is what
`metadata/relationship-types.md` §2.1 describes it as: *"the specific case
of `implements` used for the EU→national legislative chain"*.

This is the same relationship type **one level up**: an EU directive
implementing a UN convention. The vocabulary did not need extending — the
definition says *"an EU (or other higher-level) legal instrument"*, and the
parenthesis, written in Batch 3, turns out to have anticipated this exact
case two years before there was an instrument to use it on.

**No new relationship type was added for the UN → EU step.** That is a
genuine reusability result and worth stating: the ontology was built for a
three-level world and had the right edge waiting.

## Why this closes the Atlas's largest structural gap

`validation/reports.md` (Batch 15) recorded that `UN → anything` was 0 and
called it *"the single most significant structural gap in the Atlas"*. Five
country batches did not touch it.

This chain and the statistics bridge close it together, but they close
different halves:

| | Statistics bridge | This chain |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | organisational — who sits in which forum | **legislative — what obliges whom** |
| Edge into the UN layer | `participates-in` | `implements-requirement-from` |
| Reaches the national layer? | through [[EU-ESS]] membership | **yes, directly — `applies-in` to five countries** |

The legislative half is the one the README's target architecture actually
describes: *"An international principle can influence an EU strategy. An EU
regulation can lead to national implementation."* This is the first complete
instance of it.

## The national transpositions are not identified

Five `applies-in` edges are asserted, and **not one transposing national
instrument is named.** Every evidence string says so.

This is the same discipline applied to the Open Data Directive, where
Belgium and France remain open gaps and only three of five countries are
closed. The Atlas asserts that the directive applies — which follows from
its being a directive — and refuses to guess which national act does the
work.

There is a specific trap here worth recording for whoever picks this up:
several countries have general **open-government** or
**freedom-of-information** acts already in the Atlas ([[NL-WOO]],
[[ES-LEY-37-2007]]) that cover access to public information broadly. Those
are **not** the environmental information transpositions, any more than the
2016 open data acts in Belgium and France were the Open Data Directive
transpositions. The resemblance is exactly what makes the trap work.
