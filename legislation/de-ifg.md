# Informationsfreiheitsgesetz (IFG)

> **Sourcing caveat.** This entity was compiled from search-engine results
> only; the cited pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. See
> `discovery/unresolved.md` and `progress/current-batch.md`.

## Description

The IFG came into force on **1 January 2006**. Under **§ 1 IFG** every
person has a right of access to official information vis-à-vis federal
authorities. The right is **unconditional and general**: no special
interest need be demonstrated.

Its stated purpose is to make federal administrative action more
transparent through easier access to information, thereby strengthening
citizens' effective exercise of democratic participation rights and
improving control and acceptance of state action.

The right is not absolute. **§§ 3 to 6 IFG** allow it to be restricted by
public and private interests, and authorities must justify any such
exception.

[[DE-BFDI]] handles complaints from people who consider their IFG right to
have been violated.

## Scope: federal only

The IFG binds **federal** authorities. Access to information held by Land
and municipal bodies is governed by Land legislation, none of which is an
Atlas entity — the same federal gap recorded on [[DE-GEOZG]] and
[[DE-BFDI]].

The Dutch counterpart [[NL-WOO]] has no such limitation, which makes the
two acts less comparable than their names suggest. `related_entities`
records the association for navigation; **no relationship is asserted**,
here as with every other pair of national instruments in this batch that
share a subject but not a parent.

## Open data and freedom of information are separate here

Germany keeps the two regimes in different statutes: the IFG governs
**access on request** to official information, while [[DE-DNG]] governs
**proactive publication and re-use** of public sector data under
[[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]]. There is no relationship between them in the
Atlas because no source read draws one, and because they descend from
different origins — the IFG is domestic transparency law, the DNG is an EU
transposition.

This differs from the Dutch arrangement, where [[NL-WOO]] and [[NL-WHO]]
are likewise separate but sit closer together in framing. The contrast is
noted, not modelled.
