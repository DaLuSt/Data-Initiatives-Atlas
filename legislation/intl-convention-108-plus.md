## Description

The 2018 modernisation of [[INTL-CONVENTION-108]] — **adopted, ratified by
34 states, and binding nobody.**

## ⚠ Eight years old and not in force

This is the fact the entity exists to make visible.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 10 October 2018 | Opened for signature in Strasbourg |
| — | Entry into force requires ratification by all parties, or by **38** |
| 15 May 2026 | **Moldova becomes the 34th** to ratify |

**Four ratifications short, after nearly eight years.** The Committee of
Convention 108 has strongly encouraged all state parties to sign and ratify
as soon as possible, and the Council of Europe has published a specific
appeal to EU member states to do so.

Compare the transposition delays the Atlas already records — Belgium at
twenty-nine months on [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]], the Netherlands at three
years — and this is a different order of thing. Those instruments were in
force and being breached. This one has never come into force at all.

## A status value had to be added for it

`metadata/controlled-vocabularies.md` had no word for this.

- `proposed` would have called a treaty **34 sovereign states have ratified**
  a proposal.
- `active` would have called a treaty that is **not in force** operative law.

**`adopted`** was added: formally adopted, not yet in force. It is the third
vocabulary addition in as many batches, after `proposes-to-supersede` and
`amends`, and it meets the same test each of those met — an existing value
would have had to assert something untrue.

## What it changes

Against the 1981 text, Convention 108+ adds:

- consolidated **proportionality** and **data minimisation** requirements
- controller obligations on **transparency**, **accountability**, **privacy
  by design and by default**, and **risk assessment**
- **data breach notification** without delay, to the supervisory authority
  and to affected data subjects
- the substance of [[INTL-CONVENTION-108-PROTOCOL]], folded into the
  Convention rather than left as a separate instrument

The list is recognisably the [[EU-GDPR]]'s, which is the point: the
modernisation was drafted alongside the GDPR to keep the global instrument
and the regional one compatible.

## Why a non-binding treaty still matters

[[EU-GDPR]] Recital 105 makes accession to Convention 108 a factor in
adequacy assessments. Convention 108+ raises what accession means. A state
seeking an EU adequacy decision and having no prospect of EU membership can
reach for this instrument, and several outside Europe have —
[[MU]] Mauritius has ratified the amending protocol.

That is the mechanism by which a Council of Europe treaty shapes data
protection law in jurisdictions that will never be bound by EU law.
