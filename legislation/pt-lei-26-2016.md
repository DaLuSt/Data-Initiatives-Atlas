# Lei n.º 26/2016 (LADA)

> **Sourcing caveat.** Compiled from search-engine results only; the cited
> pages were confirmed to exist but were not read. `verification:
> search-only`.

## Description

Portugal's act of **22 August 2016** on **access to administrative and
environmental information and the re-use of administrative documents** — the
LADA. Its **third amendment** approved general principles on open data and
transposed [[EU-OPEN-DATA-DIRECTIVE]].

## One act for freedom of information *and* open data

This is the structural point, and it distinguishes Portugal from every other
transposition in the Atlas.

Elsewhere, access to information and re-use of public sector information sit
in **separate instruments**: the Netherlands has the Woo and [[NL-WHO]];
Germany has [[DE-IFG]] and [[DE-IWG]]; Ireland has a Freedom of Information
Act and, separately, [[IE-PSI-REGULATIONS-2021]].

Portugal legislates them **together**, and adds environmental information —
the [[UN-AARHUS]] subject matter — into the same act. So one Portuguese
statute covers what three instruments cover in Germany.

## ⚠ A conflict between sources, resolved toward the specific one

A general European survey described **Ireland and Portugal** as the two
member states with *standalone* legislation dedicated to open data, against
Belgium, France and Spain which amended existing law.

The Portugal-specific sources say otherwise: Portugal transposed **by
amending Lei n.º 26/2016**, which is precisely the "amend existing law"
pattern.

The Atlas follows the specific source over the general one, and records the
conflict rather than silently picking a side. Ireland's standalone
instrument is confirmed independently — [[IE-PSI-REGULATIONS-2021]] exists
and is cited from the Irish Statute Book — so the general survey is right
about Ireland and appears to be wrong about Portugal.

## Not modelled

- **Which amendment**, and when. The sources say "third amendment" without
  giving its number or date, so `valid_from` on the relationship is null
  rather than a guessed 2021 or 2022.
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138** on high-value
  datasets, named by the sources. It applies across the Union and is not an
  Atlas entity — a gap that affects every member state, not just Portugal.
- The **CADA**, Portugal's access-to-documents commission, which this act
  constitutes.
