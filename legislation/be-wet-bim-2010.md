# Wet van 4 februari 2010 betreffende de methoden voor het verzamelen van gegevens

> **Created 2026-09-06**, closing a gap [[BE-WIV-1998]] flagged: "Belgium
> appears with an organic act and an oversight act but no authorisation
> regime." Two of the BIM-commissie's own official texts —
> etaamb.openjustice.be's copy of the 2010 act and the commission's own
> Rules of Procedure — were read directly and agree.

## Description

Confirmed by reading etaamb.openjustice.be's own text of the law directly:
the **BIM-wet** introduces "specifieke en uitzonderlijke methoden" —
specific and exceptional data-collection methods — into Belgian
intelligence law, inserting new articles (including **Article 18/2**)
into [[BE-WIV-1998]] rather than replacing it. It establishes "een
bestuurlijke commissie... belast met het toezicht op de in artikel 18/2
bedoelde specifieke en uitzonderlijke methoden" — an administrative
commission responsible for oversight of these methods — now [[BE-BIM-COMMISSIE]].

## Binding authorisation, not mere opinion

Confirmed by reading the Act's own text directly: the head of service may
authorise an exceptional method only "na eensluidend advies van de
commissie" (after the commission's **conforming/unanimous opinion**) — a
binding pre-authorisation gate, not the advisory role [[FR-CNCTR]] plays
for France. This places Belgium's regime structurally closer to
[[NL-TIB]]'s binding decision than to the French model.

## Not modelled

- The **Royal Decree of 12 October 2010** implementing the Act, cited as
  a complementary instrument in the commission's own Rules of Procedure.
- The Act's full substantive content — the specific and exceptional
  methods it defines, beyond the commission's authorisation role.

## Relationships

- `amends` [[BE-WIV-1998]] — inserts the special-methods regime into the
  organic law rather than constituting a freestanding one.
