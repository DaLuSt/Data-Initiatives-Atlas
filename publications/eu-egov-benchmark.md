description: >
  Annual study published by the European Commission (produced under
  contract by Capgemini, with Sogeti and IDC) evaluating the provision and
  delivery of eGovernment services in 35 countries — the 27 EU member
  states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Albania, Montenegro, North
  Macedonia, Serbia and Türkiye — though the 2025 edition's own headline EU
  publications foreground the EU27 and do not themselves name the eight
  non-EU countries; a 2025-edition country factsheet confirms Norway's
  continued inclusion. The 2025 edition assessed 14,104 government websites
  relating to nine key life events using 20 indicators and 51 survey
  questions, evaluated by an EU-wide network of mystery shoppers in November
  2024, and reports against three dimensions: online service delivery,
  interoperability signifiers, and user-friendly portals. It feeds the
  digital public services measurement used in the Digital Decade monitoring.

## Description

The Commission's annual study of how European public administrations deliver
services online, produced under contract by Capgemini with Sogeti and IDC
(confirmed by reading the Capgemini press release directly, 2026-08-28).
The **2025 edition** assessed **14,104 government websites** covering
**nine key life events**, using **20 indicators** and **51 survey
questions**, evaluated by an EU-wide network of **mystery shoppers** in
**November 2024** — all five figures confirmed by reading Capgemini's dated
2025 press release directly, which also notes as "new in the 2025
eGovernment Benchmark" that all nine life events were measured in a single
research cycle for the first time. The study reports against three
dimensions:

- online service delivery
- interoperability signifiers
- user-friendly portals

It feeds the **digital public services** measurement that appears in
[[EU-DESI]] and the Digital Decade monitoring — confirmed by reading
digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu's DESI page directly, which describes the
Benchmark as a distinct, citizen-focused evaluation feeding the broader
DESI/Digital Decade monitoring framework.

## The 35 countries are all in this Atlas — with a caveat this pass surfaced

The benchmark's general scope, per the DESI-comparison page read directly,
is the 27 member states plus **eight non-EU countries**:

| | |
|---|---|
| EEA EFTA and EFTA | [[IS]], [[NO]], [[CH]] |
| Enlargement countries | [[AL]], [[ME]], [[MK]], [[RS]], [[TR]] |

Every one of those thirty-five is an Atlas country anchor, which is a
consequence of the European country expansion rather than a coincidence — the
Atlas took "the Council of Europe member states" as its scope rule, and the
Commission's benchmark scope sits inside it.

This pass found a wrinkle worth recording rather than smoothing over: the
2025 edition's own headline publications — read directly, both the
op.europa.eu listing and the Commission's 2025-specific library page —
state the study's scope as "the 27 European Union Member States (EU27)"
and do not themselves list the eight non-EU countries, unlike the 2022
edition's library page (also read directly), which names all eight by
country. A Norway-specific 2025 factsheet, located via search, shows
Norway was still measured that year, so the wider 35-country fieldwork
evidently continues at the factsheet level even where the EU27-focused
narrative summary does not mention it. The `measures` edges to the seven
other non-EU countries are downgraded to `confidence: low` this pass
because no equivalent 2025-specific confirmation was found for them
individually — a real, if modest, weakening of a claim the entity
previously stated flatly.

That coverage is the reason this entity is worth more than its sources: it is
the first thing in the Atlas whose stated scope is a **set of countries the
Atlas already holds in full**, and it names which of them the Commission
measures together.

## Publisher and contractor

The benchmark is a **Commission publication** — the 2025 edition is in the
Publications Office catalogue, confirmed by reading it directly — carried
out under contract by **Capgemini, with Sogeti and IDC** (confirmed by
reading Capgemini's 2025 press release directly this pass, which names all
three; the prior text said "Capgemini and partners" without naming them).

**No entity is created for Capgemini, Sogeti or IDC.** A private
contractor named as the executor of one study is not an Atlas subject, and
creating one would put a commercial organisation into a graph of public
bodies on the strength of a procurement.

## The 35 `measures` edges — now at mixed confidence

As with [[EU-DESI]], this entity carries a `measures` edge to every country
in its general scope — **35 of them**, the largest single-entity fan-out in
the Atlas outside the anchors. That count is unchanged this pass, but the
confidence behind eight of them is not: see the caveat above. The 27 EU
member states' edges, and Norway's, stay at `confidence: medium`; the
remaining seven non-EU countries' edges are now `confidence: low`.

The eight non-EU countries remain the interesting half. `EU-DESI` measures
the Union; this benchmark measures the Union **plus** three EFTA states and
five enlargement countries, and the Atlas holds all eight as anchors. So the
difference between the two publications' scopes is visible as a difference
in their edges rather than as a sentence in a description — though this
pass shows that difference is asserted with more confidence for some
countries (Norway, EU27) than for others (the remaining six) given what
was actually found this pass.
