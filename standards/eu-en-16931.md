## Description

EN 16931 is the European standard for electronic invoicing, **developed by
CEN/TC 434** — confirmed by reading Wikipedia's CEN/TC 434 article directly
(2026-08-28): the committee was established in 2014 as a technical body
within [[EU-CEN]], operates through seven working groups (core semantic
data model, syntax bindings, transmission guidelines, test methodology,
among others), and published **EN 16931-1 on 28 June 2017**, available free
of charge under an agreement between CEN and the European Commission. Its
work was mandated by [[EU-EINVOICING-DIRECTIVE]] (Directive 2014/55/EU),
confirmed by reading the Commission's own "EN 16931 compliance" page
directly: public entities must be able to receive and process invoices
complying with the standard.

It defines a **semantic data model — not a file format** — describing the
information an electronic invoice can carry (invoice number and date,
supplier and customer details, line items, totals, VAT and payment terms,
per theinvoicinghub.com, read directly). The previously stated "176
business terms" figure is not repeated here: none of the four sources read
this pass states an exact term count, so the number has been dropped
rather than carried forward unconfirmed.

CII syntaxes. That edge is asserted on XRechnung.

## Semantic model, not format — and why that matters here

The standard deliberately does not say what an invoice file looks like. It
says what an invoice **means**: which business terms exist, what they
signify, how they relate.

That is the same choice [[INTL-DCAT]] makes for dataset metadata, and it is
why national specifications like XRechnung exist at all — a semantic standard
needs a syntax binding and a national profile before anyone can send an
invoice with it.

## Not modelled

- **CEN/TC 434**, a committee rather than a body.
- The **UBL and CII syntax bindings**, and the CIUS/extension mechanism.
- Other national CIUSes — Italy's, France's and the Peppol BIS profile.
