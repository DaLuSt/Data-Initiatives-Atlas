# EN 16931 — Electronic invoicing

## Description

EN 16931 is the European standard for electronic invoicing, **issued by
[[EU-CEN]] in 2017** under [[EU-EINVOICING-DIRECTIVE]], and developed by
CEN/TC 434.

It defines a **semantic data model — not a file format** — comprising **176
business terms** describing the information an electronic invoice can carry.

## The first standard the Atlas holds that CEN maintains

[[EU-CEN]] has had national members pointing at it since the Germany batch,
and now ten of them. What it has never had is a **standard**.

The Atlas recorded the same gap for national bodies: [[GB-BSI]] participates
in five standardisation organisations "while maintaining nothing the
repository holds", and the four bodies added in the structural-fixes batch
carry the same note. [[DE-DIN]] got its first inbound specification edge in
the data-spaces batch, via DIN SPEC 27070 on [[INTL-IDS-RAM]].

EN 16931 is the first for CEN, and it arrives with the directive that
commissioned it.

`discovery/research-queue.md` has carried this as the **highest-value German
item** since the Germany batch, because it gives the Atlas a **standards-body
EU→national chain** and nothing else did.

[[DE-XRECHNUNG]] is a **CIUS** — a Core Invoice Usage Specification — of
EN 16931 for German business-to-government invoicing, supporting both UBL and
CII syntaxes. That edge is asserted on XRechnung.

## Semantic model, not format — and why that matters here

The standard deliberately does not say what an invoice file looks like. It
says what an invoice **means**: which business terms exist, what they
signify, how they relate.

That is the same choice [[INTL-DCAT]] makes for dataset metadata, and it is
why national specifications like XRechnung exist at all — a semantic standard
needs a syntax binding and a national profile before anyone can send an
invoice with it.

## Not modelled

- **CEN/TC 434**, a committee rather than a body.
- The **UBL and CII syntax bindings**, and the CIUS/extension mechanism.
- Other national CIUSes — Italy's, France's and the Peppol BIS profile.
