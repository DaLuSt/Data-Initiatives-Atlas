# Tech Sovereignty Package

## Description

Confirmed by reading the European Commission's own page directly: "Tech
sovereignty is Europe's ability to act independently in the digital
world by developing and controlling key technologies, data, and
infrastructure, while reducing reliance on non-EU providers." **On 3
June 2026**, "in a major shift in its approach to technology," the
Commission "adopted an ambitious set of measures to bolster the EU's
digital autonomy" — the Tech Sovereignty Package.

The Commission's own stated rationale: "The European Union currently
relies on non-EU countries for over 80% of key digital products,
services, infrastructure, and intellectual property." Commission
President Ursula von der Leyen had flagged tech sovereignty as a
priority in the 2025 State of the Union address.

## Four named components

Confirmed directly, the package itself comprises:

- **Two legislative proposals**: the **Chips Act 2.0** and the **Cloud
  and AI Development Act (CADA)**.
- The **EU Open Source Strategy**.
- A **Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy**.

None of these four is modelled as its own Atlas entity — named here in
prose as this package's own components, not separately researched.

## Distinct from the broader "digital sovereignty" framing — the row's actual question

Row #39 asked whether "digital sovereignty" is a named initiative with
its own governance, or only a framing within [[EU-DIGITAL-DECADE]] and
similar documents. It is both, and the sources are precise about which
is which: the Commission's own Tech Sovereignty overview page situates
this package alongside a much wider set of pre-existing initiatives it
groups under the same policy umbrella — the AI Act, [[EU-AI-CONTINENT-ACTION-PLAN]],
[[EU-DATA-ACT]], the Digital Decade, the Chips Act, a Quantum Europe
Strategy, and a proposed Digital Networks Act among them — without
stating that the package absorbs or governs any of them. This entity
models the **specific, dated, four-component package adopted 3 June
2026**, not the wider framing. The wider list is not modelled here; each
of those items is either already a separate Atlas entity or out of
scope for this pass.
